Lesson 0005 · The platform map

Inside the garage

Bedrock rented you the finished model. SageMaker AI rents you the workshop — compute, tooling, and workflows to train, deploy, and operate models that are yours.

What the garage sells

Bedrock sold you tokens. SageMaker AI sells you the machines and the workflows: compute hours and tooling to train your own models, deploy them, and keep them operating in production. Everything orbits your code — your containers, your scripts, your data — while AWS runs the fleet underneath. Billing is per-second compute, so the meter ticks only while machines run.

First, the naming: SageMaker renamed to SageMaker AI, announced December 2024 and fully adopted by February 2025. Job postings, blogs, and old docs have been inconsistent ever since — treat the two names as one product.

The platform map

Think of the platform as a hub with your model code at the center and every service as a spoke: the IDE where you write the code, the fleets that train it, the endpoints that serve it, and the pipelines that govern it.

The IDE story

Unified Studio, generally available since March 2025, is the new IDE: it bundles the data stack — Amazon Athena, AWS Glue, Amazon Redshift, Apache Airflow — together with analytics and AI, including both Bedrock and SageMaker. Studio Classic is maintenance-only. And Notebook Instances on Amazon Linux 2 (AL2) reach end of life on June 30, 2026 — a deadline hiding in plain sight for anyone still on them.

The engine story

One thing SageMaker deliberately does not do: build its own large language model (LLM) serving engine as the primary path. The first-party containers are the open-source engines — the first SGLang deep learning container (DLC) shipped November 2025, the first-party vLLM container followed December 2025 — and you can always bring your own container (BYOC) running vLLM, SGLang, or TensorRT-LLM.

AWS's engineering goes into the infrastructure around the engines: how fast model weights load, how memory gets shared, how many models pack onto one GPU. Those three are the next three lessons — cold starts, spot training, packing. This map is the furniture; those are the jobs.

Job postings say "SageMaker AI" but old docs say "SageMaker". What's actually going on?

Same product — SageMaker renamed to SageMaker AI in December 2024, fully adopted by February 2025. Docs and job postings have been catching up at different speeds since, so the two names mean the same platform.

A team wants to train and deploy their own models with their own code. What do they buy?

The garage: Training Jobs for the heavy work, hosting for serving, Pipelines to tie it together — all on SageMaker AI, billed per-second. Bedrock sells tokens instead of machines, and UltraServers sits one layer below the platform, for teams that build the platform itself.

SageMaker hosts vLLM and SGLang rather than building its own serving engine. Where does its engineering actually go?

The platform hosts the open-source engines — SGLang's container shipped November 2025, vLLM's in December 2025 — and spends its engineering on the seam around them: how fast weights load, how memory is shared, how many models pack onto one GPU. Those three seams are the next three lessons.

Your company needs custom frontier-model training from checkpoints under a subscription. Which SageMaker AI product?

Nova Forge — generally available December 2025, roughly $100K per year — trains custom frontier models from your checkpoints. Autopilot runs AutoML on tabular data, and JumpStart is the one-click model zoo.
