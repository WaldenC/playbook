Three doors, one stack

AWS sells the same AI machine at three zoom levels. Bedrock rents you the finished brain. SageMaker rents you the workshop. UltraServers rents you the factory floor.

The stack, with pins

You've drawn this stack before — five layers, from the apps at the top to the silicon at the bottom. AWS sells into three different rungs of it.

The rent ladder

What you buy at each door, from least to most control.

Taxi — Bedrock

You buy finished models by the token; AWS picks the machine, the engine, and the driver. Start in minutes. You control almost nothing below the API.

Garage — SageMaker AI

You buy compute hours and tooling: bring your own containers, scripts, and data; AWS runs the fleet and heals broken machines. Train, deploy, monitor — the full lifecycle, your way.

Factory floor — UltraServers

You buy a whole rack as one EC2 instance — 64 or 144 Trainium chips fused into a single machine. You run your own stack end to end; AWS keeps the lights on. Total control, and every problem below the roof is yours.

The three doors nest. Bedrock's models run on Trainium UltraServers, and SageMaker rents the same racks. Different customers stand at different doors of the same building.

The one-breath rule

  • Want a model that already exists? Bedrock.
  • Want to build or own your model? SageMaker AI.
  • Want to build the machine models run on? UltraServers.

Gray zone: fine-tuning lives in both Bedrock and SageMaker. Tweak a hosted model with a knob or two → Bedrock. Train seriously — data, hyperparameters, hardware choices → SageMaker.

Try it

Which door?

A bank wants Claude behind their app, no infra team, pay per token. Which door?

Bedrock is the taxi — pay per token, someone else drives. The garage is overkill if you never touch training or containers, and the factory floor is for people who build the platform, not people who call it.

An ML team fine-tunes Llama with full control of the script and the hardware. Which door?

Full control of the training script and the instance type is the garage — SageMaker Training. Bedrock's customization gives you knobs, not the engine room. UltraServers only makes sense when you're building the platform itself.

A lab needs thousands of chips wired as one machine to train a frontier model. Which product?

A whole rack fused into one instance is exactly an UltraServer. HyperPod manages clusters of machines but doesn't change what the machines are, and Bedrock batch mode changes billing, not the machine.
