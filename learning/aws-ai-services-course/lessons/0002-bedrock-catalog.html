Lesson 0002 · The catalog

What Bedrock sells

The taxi has one meter but many fares. Bedrock is a catalog of about 200 models behind a single API: pay per token, swap models without rewiring, and your data never trains the grid.

One API, about 200 models

Open the Bedrock door and the first thing on the shelf is a catalog: about 200 models behind one Converse or InvokeModel API. Anthropic Claude (Opus and Sonnet), Meta Llama (Llama 4 Scout reads a 10-million-token context), Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen3, and Cohere all sit there, and since June 2026 OpenAI's Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) models are generally available (GA) too, served from the internal bedrock-mantle endpoint. The old assumption that Bedrock has no OpenAI is dead.

Then there is Amazon Nova, the first-party family: Micro, Lite, Pro, and Premier, with Nova 2 Lite, Pro, Sonic, and Omni landing at re:Invent 2025. Nova runs on Trainium, AWS's own silicon — the same chips the factory-floor door rents out. At the far end of the shelf, Titan is legacy; it is not why you pick Bedrock anymore.

Catalog facts: AWS Bedrock model cards and the OpenAI models GA announcement.

Pay per token

The meter reads tokens. Every call pays an input price and an output price, and output typically runs 4–10x input — generation is the expensive half of a call. Batch mode is the volume discount: roughly 50% off when you can wait for answers to come back later.

The surprising bit is what Bedrock does not charge. Claude on Bedrock costs the same as Claude on Anthropic's own API, and GPT on Bedrock matches OpenAI's first-party pricing: no markup.

Pricing model: AWS's "Bedrock or SageMaker?" decision guide (PDF) — the rent ladder from the seller's side.

Your data never trains the grid

The privacy guarantee is contractual, not a vibe: prompts and responses are never used to train models, never shared with the model providers, and not retained by default. When a customer's legal team asks whether Anthropic can see their prompts, the answer is no.

Scale

The grid's size, from AWS earnings calls: more than 125,000 customers, about 80% of the Fortune 100, and a multibillion-dollar annualized run rate. AWS says more tokens were processed in Q1 2026 than in all prior years combined.

Scale claims: AWS AI earnings-call coverage, Q1 2026.

Why one API matters

Two hundred models behind one API is a strategy, not a convenience. Swap Claude for Llama or Nova by changing one modelId field — no new SDK, no new plumbing, nothing else to rewire.

Version IDs make the swap safe. A model-version ID pins your calls to one specific model, so when Bedrock rotates in a newer Sonnet the catalog moves and your callers keep talking to the version they were tested against. You migrate on your own schedule.

What did you catch?

Which model family is first-party — built and served by AWS?

Nova is AWS's own family, running on Trainium silicon — the first-party story behind the catalog. Llama is Meta's open-weight line and Claude is Anthropic's flagship; Bedrock hosts them, it does not own them.

Why does a Bedrock call charge more per output token than per input token?

Output typically runs 4–10x the input price — generation is the expensive half of a call. And if you can wait, batch mode runs the same work at roughly 50% off.

A customer's legal team asks: does Anthropic see the prompts sent to Claude on Bedrock?

No. Prompts and responses are never used to train models, never shared with model providers, and not retained by default. The provider gets the billing for the call, not its payload.

Which statement about OpenAI on Bedrock is true?

The June 2026 GA ended the old assumption: GPT runs on the same internal engine as the rest of the catalog — the bedrock-mantle endpoint — priced at parity with OpenAI's own API.
