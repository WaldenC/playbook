Cheap machines that die

Spot training rents you idle GPUs at up to 90% off the list price — because AWS can take them back mid-run. The whole art is making an interruption cost minutes instead of days.

The discount aisle

SageMaker's Managed Spot Training is an auction for idle GPUs. At any moment part of the fleet sits unused, and the platform sells that capacity at up to 90% off the on-demand list price — real-world discounts land around 60–70%. The catch: AWS can reclaim the machine with about two minutes' notice.

Two minutes is enough time to do nothing but stop. So the design question is never whether a machine will die mid-run — it's what you lose when it does.

The knobs

A spot job declares the deal in two fields. MaxWaitTimeInSeconds is the total budget: 259200 in the snippet below means "keep trying for three days", even across repeated reclaims. CheckpointConfig names the S3 (Simple Storage Service) location where progress lands before the machines go.

Checkpoint, checkpoint, checkpoint

AWS's guidance is to checkpoint every 15–30 minutes. Then a two-minute notice costs you at most one interval of work, and the job resumes on the next free spot slot instead of restarting from zero.

The economics add up. Cinnamon AI, which trains models for Japanese businesses, cut training costs by 70% and ran 40% more daily jobs by moving to spot — same budget, more experiments.

AWS ML blog — Cinnamon AI saves 70% with Managed Spot Training.

Assume death

Checkpointing exists because long runs die. Meta reported a GPU failure roughly every three hours while training LLaMA, so a multi-day run is a survival exercise, not a computation. HyperPod's first answer is self-healing: it detects failed nodes and replaces them.

In December 2025 SageMaker went further. Checkpointless training replicates the model state peer to peer across the cluster, so when a node dies the survivors rebuild the lost copy in minutes — more than 80% less downtime — and Amazon Nova trained on tens of thousands of accelerators this way.

Elastic training, added in 2025, lets a running job grow onto spare capacity mid-run while preserving the global batch size, with a backoff that stops the job from thrashing between sizes.

AWS blog — Checkpointless and elastic training on SageMaker HyperPod.

One sentence to keep: spot makes machines cheap and unreliable; checkpoints turn unreliability into a pause; and the platform keeps retrying until the budget runs out.

Try it

Surviving the reclaim

Your 3-day training run is at hour 40 when AWS reclaims your spot instances. What does a well-built job do?

A checkpoint every 15–30 minutes means the reclaim costs you at most half an hour of work, not the 40 hours you'd lose restarting from zero — and MaxWaitTimeInSeconds keeps the job retrying on fresh spot capacity until its total budget is spent. Paying on-demand prices is the one thing spot training is meant to avoid.

You enable Managed Spot Training for a job. Which field controls the total time budget, including retries across reclaims?

MaxWaitTimeInSeconds is the wall-clock budget the job keeps trying within — 259200 seconds is three days, and reclaims eat into it like any other wait. InstanceCount only sizes the cluster, and VolumeSizeInGB only sizes the attached storage; neither one makes the job retry.

AWS sends a reclaim warning for your spot machine. How much notice do you get?

AWS gives about two minutes of warning before reclaiming a spot machine. That's why the guidance is a 15–30 minute checkpoint cadence: the most you can lose is one interval, and the job resumes on fresh spot capacity. The short notice is the whole reason the discount exists — AWS needs the machine back fast, and your job only needs to stop cleanly.

A HyperPod node dies during checkpointless training. What keeps the job alive?

In checkpointless training the model state lives replicated across the cluster — a peer-to-peer copy, not a single checkpoint file. When a node dies, the survivors rebuild the missing copy in minutes, which is how Amazon reports more than 80% less training downtime and how Nova trained on tens of thousands of accelerators. Restarting the job, or swapping a node with no replica anywhere, is the old world.
